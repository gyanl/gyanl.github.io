{%- comment -%} The stickers ringing the hero mark. The hero IS an iMessage typing indicator, so the things orbiting it are the stickers that thread would send — which is why they belong here and nowhere else on the page. Each one is placed by a direction vector rather than an angle, so the ring can be an ellipse fitted to the viewport (--ring-x by --ring-y in main.css) instead of a circle that would run off the top and bottom of a landscape window. --dx and --dy are that direction, and also the line each one travels out along as the page scrolls, so a sticker leaves the frame straight out from the centre. Roughly even around the clock, with one gap left on purpose: nothing sits where the typing bubble hangs, just up and right of the mark's centre. Bottom dead centre is the welcome note's, and the scroll cue sits below that. --shadow-x/--shadow-y are the sticker's own shadow offset, eyeballed off its --dx/--dy rather than computed from them: a downward drop with a nudge away from the centre, so the ring reads as lit from the middle and spreading out. The ones around the top of the ring carry .hero-sticker--tip-up: their notes hang above them rather than below, so a note does not drop across the mark in the middle. --art-h is the file's own pixel height, and it is what sizes the sticker: one factor in main.css turns a source pixel into an on-screen one, so every sticker is drawn at its own resolution times the same number. Export a sticker larger to have it sit larger in the ring — there is no figure here to keep in step with it, and the width follows from the art's aspect. It replaced a --sticker-scale per sticker, roughly sqrt(0.8 / aspect) each, which was correcting for every cut-out being drawn to a COMMON height: at an equal height a 2.6:1 sticker draws two and a half times the width of a square one and swamps the ring. Those numbers described the sizing rather than the artwork, and each new sticker needed one derived for it. The two wide ones are still placed left and right, where the ellipse is widest and there is room for them. The ring is NOT aria-hidden any more. Each sticker is a real button — hover on a mouse, tap on a touchscreen — and a focusable control inside an aria-hidden subtree is a tab stop nobody is told about. The button carries the note as its accessible name, so it is read once; the visible bubble is hidden from AT to avoid saying it twice, and the art keeps alt="" because the note already describes it. {%- endcomment -%}
